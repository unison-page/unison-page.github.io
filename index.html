01 Overview

One interaction, one state

A complete grasp state contains hand pose, the surface region that touches the object, and the load distributed over that region. Separate prediction pipelines lose many of the physical constraints that connect these signals.

Pose, contact, and pressure evolve together throughout approach, contact formation, load bearing, and release. Their recovery from one camera is difficult because fingers and contact interfaces are frequently occluded. Pressure also leaves few direct visual cues, and similar hand appearances can represent different loads when object mass, shape, or friction changes.

UNISON formulates video-conditioned hand understanding as generation of a coupled trajectory. The trajectory contains a compact articulated pose, 162 taxel-level contact values, and 162 taxel-level pressure values at every frame. A shared state-space model exchanges evidence across state components and time. Local visual context supplies motion evidence under occlusion. Optional language, physical attributes, and object shape cues further reduce ambiguity when the video alone is insufficient.

The resulting representation provides a common interface for grasp analysis, tactile inference, and physical skill learning. It also supports transfer across camera viewpoints because contact and pressure remain anchored to the hand surface.

Unified state representation

Viewpoint-independent tactile coordinates

The 34-dimensional pose state describes global motion and finger articulation without committing to a specific mesh. Contact and pressure share a fixed 162-taxel hand-surface layout, so tactile predictions remain anatomically meaningful across camera views and can be calibrated to different hand embodiments.

The taxels are organized into anatomical regions and connected through a fixed graph. This structure preserves local neighborhoods along each finger and across the palm. It also allows the model to reason with compact regional tokens while retaining dense taxel-level outputs for contact and pressure.

02 UNISON-Sim

Unified hand-state supervision

Physical labels at video scale

Real capture rarely provides RGB, articulated pose, dense contact, and pressure together. UNISON-Sim closes that gap using staged dexterous grasp trajectories replayed in MuJoCo with a UR10e arm and Shadow Hand. Successful sequences are extended through holding and return motions, which cover approach, contact formation, load bearing, and object release. Every retained timestep exports synchronized RGB, a 34-dimensional articulated pose, 162-taxel contact, and 162-taxel pressure from the same physical state.

A filtering pipeline retains complete episodes that satisfy grasp success and visibility checks. Camera placement, textures, object appearance, and illumination are randomized while the underlying interaction state remains unchanged. This creates visual diversity without weakening the alignment between observation and physical supervision.

The final benchmark contains 45,124 episodes over 2,356 object instances. Object-disjoint train, validation, and test partitions measure generalization to unseen instances. Each episode can also include natural-language descriptions, structured mass, size, and friction attributes, together with a BPS shape prior when the object mesh is available.

03 Method

Conditional state-space Flow Matching

Keep each state’s geometry, reason in one space

UNISON performs conditional Flow Matching directly over the unified state sequence. Pose follows a compact single-token path. Contact and pressure follow topology-aware tactile paths that preserve their hand-surface organization. The resulting tokens enter a shared State DiT where temporal evidence and cross-state dependencies are modeled jointly.

A frozen DINOv2 backbone extracts frame-wise visual features and lightweight LoRA modules adapt its attention projections to hand-object video. State tokens attend to a local temporal window, which preserves nearby finger motion and contact transitions. A frozen UMT5 encoder supplies global language context.

Structured mass, size, and friction values are encoded as typed slots with availability and uncertainty information. Optional BPS distances describe object geometry. Bidirectional attention aligns visual and text tokens before they enter the State DiT, while the static conditions control adaptive normalization and attention gates.

01

Modality-aware context

Nearby visual tokens preserve motion cues during occlusion. Text supplies sequence-level object and action semantics. Numeric attributes provide direct physical values, while BPS geometry constrains feasible hand configurations and tactile regions.

02

Cross-state exchange

One pose token and regional contact and pressure tokens attend within the same transformer. Pose supplies articulated geometry. Contact identifies support regions. Pressure receives evidence from both geometry and contact through shared self-attention.

03

State-balanced training

Flow Matching losses are computed independently for pose, contact, and pressure before weighted aggregation. This balances the compact pose state with the larger tactile fields and keeps supervision effective for all three components.

Physics-motivated order

Pose → contact → pressure

Hand geometry is most visible, contact must be compatible with that geometry, and pressure is supported by contacted regions while also depending on the grasp phase and object properties. UNISON follows this ordering explicitly in three State DiT passes.

  1. PoseResolve articulated geometry from the noisy unified state.
  2. ContactUse the updated pose as context for active hand-surface regions.
  3. PressureUse both pose and contact to infer load distribution.
04 UNISON-Sim Results

Unified prediction in motion

Consistent geometry, support, and load

Under one evaluation protocol, UNISON consistently improves all three state components over specialized baselines. It yields more accurate hand geometry, stronger contact overlap and temporal consistency, and more faithful pressure reconstruction. Improvements are especially clear in the finger regions that determine contact support and pressure localization. The pressure predictions also show better spatial structure and load placement.

Controlled ablations trace these gains to unified supervision and structured information exchange. Joint pose and pressure training improves both states over their single-state variants. Enabling cross-state self-attention produces a clear improvement in pressure overlap under the same supervision and model capacity.

Restoring hierarchical pose, contact, and pressure generation provides another consistent gain and produces the strongest overall result. The progression supports the central design. Joint supervision provides complementary evidence, cross-state attention exchanges that evidence, and ordered generation follows the physical dependency among the states.

05 Real-World Adaptation

UNISON-S2R visual transfer

Adapt the camera interface, preserve the state prior

Simulation supplies complete state supervision. Real video introduces changes in hand appearance, background, lighting, camera response, motion blur, and occlusion statistics. Dense real pose, contact, and pressure labels remain difficult to collect at scale. UNISON therefore keeps the coupled state generator frozen and adapts only a real-domain LoRA inside the visual encoder.

The simulated branch acts as a frozen teacher and the real branch acts as a student. Both branches feed visual tokens into the same frozen State DiT under shared auxiliary conditions. Token alignment and generator-level velocity consistency teach the real visual encoder to produce features that remain compatible with the simulation-trained state prior.

This restricted update targets the visual source of the domain gap and protects the learned relationships among pose, contact, and pressure. LoRA regularization also keeps the real adapter close to its simulation initialization when paired visual data is limited.

UNISON-S2R contains 4,820 paired sequence-view records from 488 source demonstrations. Real human-hand clips and simulated dexterous-hand clips share the same action segment, frame count, frame rate, and viewpoint index. Four synchronized cameras cover right-hand, left-hand, and bimanual interactions across 61 action primitives.

The paired records establish direct visual correspondence across domains without providing dense real interaction-state labels. They therefore support adapter training while keeping real tactile measurements reserved for independent evaluation.
